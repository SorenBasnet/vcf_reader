# Northstar Analytics — Company Policies

**Document ID:** NSP-001
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026
**Status:** Fictional / For Testing Only

---

## 1. Remote Work Policy

Northstar Analytics allows employees to work remotely up to three days per week. Employees working remotely are expected to remain available during their team's designated core hours of 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM Central Time.

Employees must have a reliable internet connection and a workspace that allows them to perform their responsibilities securely and professionally.

Remote employees are responsible for protecting company information when working outside the office. Confidential documents should not be displayed where unauthorized individuals can view them, and company devices should not be left unattended in public locations.

Managers may approve temporary fully remote arrangements when business circumstances require them.

---

## 2. Information Security Policy

All employees must protect company data from unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, or destruction.

Company accounts must use strong passwords and multi-factor authentication when available. Employees must never share their passwords with coworkers, contractors, customers, or other third parties.

Sensitive company information should only be stored using company-approved systems. Employees should not upload confidential datasets to personal cloud storage services.

If an employee believes that company information has been exposed or that an account may have been compromised, the employee must notify the security team as soon as possible.

Security incidents should be reported even when the employee is uncertain whether an actual breach occurred.

---

## 3. Acceptable Use of Company Computers

Company-owned computers and other electronic devices are primarily intended for business purposes.

Employees may use company devices for limited personal activities as long as those activities do not interfere with work responsibilities, violate company policies, consume excessive resources, or create security risks.

Employees may not install unauthorized software on company-managed computers. Software required for work should be obtained through approved installation procedures.

Employees should not use company computers to access illegal content, distribute malicious software, attempt to bypass security controls, or conduct activities that could damage the company's reputation.

The IT department may monitor company-owned devices for security, maintenance, troubleshooting, and compliance purposes.

---

## 4. Data Retention Policy

Northstar Analytics retains business records only for as long as necessary to satisfy operational, legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements.

Employees should store important business records in approved company systems rather than personal storage locations.

Temporary files, duplicate records, and unnecessary copies of sensitive information should be deleted when they are no longer required.

Certain records may be subject to mandatory retention periods. Employees must not intentionally delete or modify records that are subject to a legal hold or investigation.

Questions regarding retention requirements should be directed to the compliance team.

---

## 5. Artificial Intelligence Policy

Employees may use approved artificial intelligence tools to improve productivity, generate drafts, summarize information, analyze non-sensitive data, and assist with software development.

Employees must not enter confidential customer information, private employee information, proprietary source code, credentials, or other restricted information into an AI system unless the system has been explicitly approved for that type of information.

AI-generated material must be reviewed by a qualified employee before it is used in a customer-facing product, important business decision, publication, or production software system.

Employees remain responsible for the accuracy and appropriateness of work produced with AI assistance.

AI tools should be treated as productivity tools rather than authoritative sources of factual information.

---

## 6. Software Development Policy

Production software must be stored in the company's approved version-control system.

Developers should create feature branches for significant changes and submit code for review before merging changes into production branches.

Production credentials, API keys, passwords, and other secrets must never be committed to source-code repositories.

Automated tests should be created for important functionality whenever practical.

Changes affecting sensitive systems should receive additional review from an authorized engineer.

Developers are responsible for documenting significant architectural decisions and maintaining accurate technical documentation.

---

## 7. Employee Privacy Policy

Northstar Analytics respects the privacy of its employees.

Employee information should only be accessed by individuals who require that information to perform legitimate business responsibilities.

Managers should not request personal information from employees unless the information is necessary for an appropriate business purpose.

Employees may request access to certain personal information maintained by the company, subject to applicable laws and company procedures.

Personal information must not be shared outside the company unless authorized or required by law.

---

## 8. Password and Authentication Policy

Employees must use unique passwords for company accounts.

Passwords should not be reused across multiple company systems when those systems support separate authentication credentials.

Multi-factor authentication is required for systems designated as high-risk by the security team.

Authentication tokens, recovery codes, and security keys must be protected in the same manner as passwords.

If an employee loses a security device or believes authentication credentials have been compromised, the incident must be reported immediately.

---

## 9. Incident Response Policy

A security incident includes any suspected unauthorized access, data exposure, malware infection, credential compromise, or other event that could threaten company information or systems.

Employees who discover a suspected security incident should immediately stop actions that could make the situation worse and contact the security team.

Employees should preserve relevant information when possible. They should not attempt to investigate or delete evidence unless instructed by the security team.

The security team will evaluate reported incidents, determine their severity, and coordinate appropriate response activities.

Employees are expected to cooperate with incident-response investigations.

---

## 10. Employee Travel Policy

Employees traveling for approved business purposes may be reimbursed for reasonable transportation, lodging, and meal expenses.

Travel expenses should be documented using receipts or other approved documentation.

Employees should select reasonably priced transportation and lodging options consistent with the requirements of the business trip.

Personal expenses are not reimbursable unless explicitly approved under a company program.

Travel involving sensitive company equipment or information requires employees to follow the company's information-security requirements.

---

## 11. Expense Reimbursement Policy

Employees may request reimbursement for reasonable expenses incurred while performing authorized business activities.

Expense reports should normally be submitted within 30 days of the expense.

Each expense report should identify the business purpose of the expense and include supporting documentation when required.

Managers are responsible for reviewing expense reports for accuracy and business appropriateness.

Fraudulent expense claims may result in disciplinary action.

---

## 12. Workplace Conduct Policy

Employees are expected to interact with coworkers, customers, contractors, and business partners professionally and respectfully.

Harassment, threats, discrimination, intimidation, and intentional disruptive behavior are not permitted.

Employees should raise workplace concerns through appropriate management or reporting channels.

Retaliation against an employee who makes a good-faith report or participates in an investigation is prohibited.

The company may investigate reported violations and take appropriate action when misconduct is confirmed.

---

## 13. Policy Exceptions

Employees may request an exception to a company policy when following the standard policy would create an unusual business or technical problem.

Exception requests should identify the policy involved, explain the reason for the request, describe the proposed alternative, and identify any associated risks.

Policy exceptions must be approved by an authorized manager or department before the employee proceeds.

Temporary exceptions should include an expiration date.

Security-related exceptions may require additional approval from the security team.

---

## 14. Policy Updates

Company policies may be modified when business requirements, technology, laws, regulations, or security risks change.

Employees are responsible for reviewing policy updates that apply to their responsibilities.

The most recent approved version of a policy takes precedence over previous versions.

Questions about the interpretation of a policy should be directed to the department responsible for maintaining that policy.
